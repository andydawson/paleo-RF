# Domain precedent for the CV redesign, and where AIC fits

**Summary**

- Published forest-albedo studies do compare candidate regressions and use AIC; the earlier redesign should have cited them.
- The closest albedo precedent combines candidate-model comparison with transfer tests between geographical areas.
- Nested assessment has applied ecological and palaeoclimate precedent, but I found no direct albedo example of the proposed directional nesting.
- Withholding environmental extremes has palaeoecological precedent; it does not establish geographical independence.
- Simulation tests and environmental-distance diagnostics also have domain precedent, but the proposed implementations are project-specific.
- AIC assesses predictive fit under the calibration setting; it does not establish accuracy for novel cover mixtures or identify cover separately from location.
- I recommend REML within fits, target-specific CV for choosing configurations, separate assessment, and conditional AIC reported alongside.
- No files were changed. “None found” below describes this search, not proof that no precedent exists.

## 2. AIC and the other criteria

### What the pipeline actually does

The current code implements the B4 arrangement:

1. [Script 4](/home/expert/Documents/contracting/Andria/paleo-RF-wt/comparison/scripts/4_calibration_model.R:43) fits the eight-model ladder using `comparison_method`, currently **ML**, and writes monthly AIC comparisons.
2. The prediction model is selected through [config.R](/home/expert/Documents/contracting/Andria/paleo-RF-wt/comparison/config.R:69), currently `model_id = "mod8"`. **The script does not automatically select the lowest-AIC model.**
3. Script 4 fits that configured model separately by **REML** for prediction.
4. [Script 5](/home/expert/Documents/contracting/Andria/paleo-RF-wt/comparison/scripts/5_calibration_eval.R:318) reports the ladder’s AIC again in its **November-only** comparison block and saves the separately fitted prediction model for downstream use.

Thus “AIC comparison followed by REML prediction fitting” is accurate; “automatic AIC selection” is not.

### What each criterion tells us

| Criterion | What it estimates or measures | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| **AIC** | Relative expected predictive information loss under the fitted sampling setting, using likelihood fit with a complexity penalty. In mgcv, conditional AIC uses the data likelihood at penalized coefficient estimates and effective degrees of freedom, with a smoothing-uncertainty correction where available. Lower is preferred among comparable candidates. | Error for novel cover mixtures, geographical transfer, or correct allocation between cover and location. It assesses the predictive distribution, so it need not rank models like squared-error CV. [Wood et al., §5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.03864). |
| **BIC** | A fit–complexity criterion whose ordinary derivation approximates Bayesian model evidence for large samples. Its usual penalty is \(\log(n)\) per parameter, versus AIC’s 2, so it generally penalizes complexity more strongly. | An extrapolation score. Mechanically applying `BIC()` to a penalized GAM should not be assumed to retain every property of ordinary fixed-dimensional Bayesian model comparison. [R documentation](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/stats/html/AIC.html); [Schwarz, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176344136). |
| **ML/REML smoothing scores** | Criteria for estimating smoothing/variance parameters. REML accounts for estimation of the unpenalized part when estimating those parameters. In mgcv, the reported score is a negative marginal or restricted log likelihood. | Held-out prediction error. **REML scores should not compare models with different fixed/unpenalized structures.** [Model-selection documentation](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/mgcv/html/gam.selection.html); [summary documentation](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/mgcv/html/summary.gam.html). |
| **GCV** | An approximate leave-one-out prediction-error criterion used to choose smoothness. It adjusts fitting error for effective model complexity. | Spatial-block or cover-tail validation. Its name does not mean that geographically separated or environmentally novel observations have been withheld. [mgcv guidance](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/mgcv/html/gam.selection.html). |
| **Deviance explained** | The fraction of null-model deviance removed on the fitting data. It describes how much better the fitted model represents those observations than the null model. | A complexity-adjusted model-selection criterion or an estimate of transfer performance. [Definition](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/mgcv/html/summary.gam.html). |
| **Cross-validation** | Prediction performance for the withholding arrangement, loss function and weighting actually used. Random, geographical and cover-tail splits ask different questions. | Performance under every historical condition, or identification of the true vegetation effect. If CV also chooses the model, its winning score needs separate assessment to avoid selection optimism. [Schratz et al., author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.11266); [Wenger & Olden](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2041-210X.2011.00170.x). |

**The REML/AIC distinction matters.** Conditional `AIC()` on a REML-fitted GAM is not the restricted-likelihood score. The prohibition on comparing REML scores across differing fixed structures does **not** automatically invalidate conditional AIC from those fits. The existing ML ladder is a defensible convention, not a prerequisite for obtaining valid conditional AIC.

The correction used by mgcv accounts for estimating smoothing parameters; it is **not AICc**, the familiar small-sample correction. The package applies it when the required information is available under ML/REML fitting. Comparisons should use the same response observations and compatible likelihoods; raw AIC values from different folds or months are not directly comparable measures of model quality. [Official `logLik.gam` documentation](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/mgcv/html/logLik.gam.html).

### Why they can disagree

They optimize different things. AIC rewards predictive likelihood across the calibration observations; the proposed CV rewards historically weighted squared prediction error in deliberately difficult cover conditions. BIC applies a stronger complexity penalty. REML estimates smoothing through a likelihood criterion. Deviance explained measures fit without a selection penalty. Different rankings can therefore be legitimate, even when every fit converges.

There is a second difference: **the prediction task changes**. A flexible location surface may improve modern albedo prediction substantially. That improvement does not tell us whether the cover response remains correct when cover changes at the same location.

For this pipeline, the practical concern is that two models can predict similar total modern albedo while assigning different amounts to cover and location. Changing cover subsequently exposes that difference. **Neither AIC nor accurate total-albedo CV resolves it by itself.** This is why local contrasts and known-truth diagnostics address something additional. Zhao & Jackson’s paired-location design is a direct domain example of trying to control the setting when estimating vegetation contrasts. [Their methods](https://jacksonlab.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj20871/files/media/file/em2014.pdf).

### How to combine the criteria

| Combination | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **REML within each fit; target-specific CV chooses the configuration; conditional AIC reported alongside** | **Recommended.** Each tool has a clear role. AIC shows whether the transfer-oriented choice sacrifices modern likelihood fit; it does not veto that choice. |
| **AIC filters candidates before target-specific CV** | Not recommended here. It can discard a model that transfers better despite worse calibration fit. A full-data filter would also expose outer-test responses to selection. Any such filter would have to occur entirely inside each outer training set and be assessed as part of the procedure. |
| **AIC chooses inside each outer training set; outer holdouts assess the result** | A coherent simpler alternative that needs no inner CV search. But it selects for calibration performance, then tests transfer. It does not directly optimize the approved cover-extrapolation objective. |

There is domain precedent for combining rather than choosing between these tools. **Bright uses AIC for forest-albedo regression selection. Pirzamanbein reports DIC beside CV. Wenger & Olden use AIC-selected ecological models and then examine transfer using nonrandom geographical divisions.** These examples support distinct selection and assessment roles; they do not establish AIC as a substitute for the historical transfer question. [Bright supplement](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/12/2195/2015/bg-12-2195-2015-supplement.pdf), [Pirzamanbein methods](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2018EA000547), [Wenger & Olden](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2041-210X.2011.00170.x).

## Recommendation: keep, cut and change

1. **Keep REML and a bounded comparison of cover form and location capacity.** The twelve configurations are defensible as an explicit sensitivity design for their interaction, but the literature does not make that grid compulsory. Label its numerical settings as our judgement. Basis size limits available complexity; REML estimates the fitted smoothness. Do not describe every change in `k` as an independent biological model.

2. **Keep nested assessment if CV selects the configuration.** Freeze candidates, scoring, thresholds and failure rules before examining outer results. Treat the outer results as assessment of the frozen procedure on development data. Nesting cannot undo earlier use of these observations or remove geographical dependence.

3. **Add conditional AIC beside CV; do not add an AIC screening stage.** Record it from comparable fits, together with effective degrees of freedom and fit diagnostics. There is no need for a second grid of smoothing criteria or a vote among AIC, BIC, GCV and deviance explained.

4. **Keep cover-tail and geographical assessments distinct.** One spatial-block CV would be simpler, but could leave the model interpolating in cover space. Directional cover holdouts directly address the stated concern, while geographical tests expose dependence on location. Neither validates historical albedo itself.

5. **Keep recovery and distance reporting as diagnostics, with narrower claims.** The recovery experiment can reveal an inability to separate stipulated cover and location effects. Distance summaries can show where modern transfer errors worsen. Cut any interpretation that passing either establishes the historical sign or validates more extreme cover mixtures than were tested.

The simplest sound combination for the stated objective is therefore **REML for smoothing, CV for configuration choice, separate assessment of that choice, and AIC alongside it**. The additional recovery check is warranted by the specific cover–location concern, rather than by a general requirement to make model selection elaborate.
