Wood's own help examples compare REML fits with different terms by `AIC()`: `b0`, `bt`, `bs` all `method="REML"`, `AIC(b0,bt,bs)` (evidence lines 977-1003); `lr.fit`, `lr.fit1`, `lr.fit2` (1073-1091). Consequence: a referee who knows mgcv will say the justification misdescribes the criterion. **Answers to the packet's questions.**

1. *Uses.* Listed in findings 1-2 and the hand check. In the pipeline: written `scripts/4_calibration_model.R:78-88`; read `scripts/5_calibration_eval.R:244-251`, used only by `simulate()` at 257; catalogue `R/paths.R:65-66, 99, 159-166`; tests `tests/test_paths.R:54-55, 74`, `tests/test_verify_change.R:97` (paths only). Off-pipeline `scripts/6_prediction_model_spatial_eval.R:65, 72, 79, 87, 94, 100` (read), 60-103 (predict), 122-154 (simulate). Original: written `4_calibration_model.R:357-426`; read `5_calibration_eval.R:342-349` and `6_prediction_model_spatial_eval.R` as above. No AIC, BIC, likelihood ratio or `anova` on them, ever.

2. *Method for these uses.* REML. The uses are a fitted model's predictions and response simulations, i.e. estimation, for which mgcv's documentation prefers REML: `gam` help, `method` argument: "Beyond the exponential family "REML" is the default" (evidence 551-553); Details: "a Laplace approximation to REML. There is some evidence that the latter may actually be the most effective choice" (720-722); `gam.selection`: "REML and ML are less prone to local minima than the other criteria" (51-52); `logLik.gam`: "marginal likelihood underestimates variance components/oversmooths" (246-247), i.e. ML fits are smoother than REML fits, which is why their simulated means would differ from the prediction model's. `bam` accepts `"ML"` and `"REML"` with extended families (bam help, CRAN, read today), so the ML fits are computable, just not wanted. Wood (2011), JRSS B 73(1):3-36, doi:10.1111/j.1467-9868.2010.00749.x, is the source for REML's lower bias in smoothing-parameter estimation (from memory, to be checked; the abstract was not retrievable).

3. *The general rule.* Standard and citable for mixed models: nlme `anova.lme` help, "Likelihood comparisons are not meaningful for objects fit using restricted maximum likelihood and with different fixed effects" (CRAN, read today); lme4 `anova` for `merMod`, argument `refit`: "The default is TRUE to prevent the common mistake of inappropriately comparing REML-fitted models with different fixed effects, whose likelihoods are not directly comparable" (CRAN, read today); Pinheiro and Bates (2000), *Mixed-Effects Models in S and S-PLUS*, Springer, doi:10.1007/b98882, stated at p. 76 (page from a search summary, to be checked); Zuur et al. (2009), *Mixed Effects Models and Extensions in Ecology with R*, Springer, doi:10.1007/978-0-387-87458-6, ten-step protocol, ch. 4 p. 90 and ch. 5 (random structure by REML, fixed structure by ML, final model refitted by REML; section 5.7 and p. 121-122 from memory, to be checked). For GAMs: `gam.selection` (evidence 107-111) recommends comparing "GCV/UBRE/ML scores ... (REML scores should not be used to compare models with different fixed effects structures)" and 133-134 "it is probably best to use ML smoothness selection" for term selection; Wood (2017), 2nd ed., doi:10.1201/9781315370279, makes the same point in the linear-mixed-model and GAM-theory chapters (section numbers from memory, not verified). Sources on the other side: mgcv's own `AIC()` is the conditional AIC and is documented and exemplified on REML fits with different terms (finding 3); Greven and Kneib (2010), Biometrika 97(4):773-789, and Säfken, Rügamer, Kneib and Greven (2021), J. Stat. Software 99(8), doi:10.18637/jss.v099.i08 (abstract read today), develop the conditional AIC for REML-fitted mixed models precisely because the marginal criterion is unsuitable; Gurka (2006), Am. Stat. 60(1):19-26, doi:10.1198/000313006X90396, argues REML-based information criteria can be used for fixed-effect selection when the restricted likelihood's constant is handled correctly (abstract not retrievable; conclusion from memory, to be checked). Would this change the 14 October ladder decision? Not necessarily: fitting all compared models by ML follows `gam.selection` and the mixed-model protocol, is conservative, and is already run; but it is not *forced* by the AIC the pipeline computes, the recorded reason should say so, and the January reversal (finding 6) shows the choice matters, which is a question for Andria, not a settled point.

4. *The explanation.* Accurate: what REML removes, REML as mgcv's choice for a model used to predict, and that the seven's comparison never uses the REML score. To correct: (a) "comparing them by AIC is invalid" should read "comparing their REML *scores* is invalid; mgcv's `AIC()` uses the penalized log-likelihood and corrected degrees of freedom, works for REML or ML fits, and Wood's own examples compare REML fits by it; what matters is that all compared models are fitted by the same method"; (b) add that the seven's figure *does* depend on the fitting method through the smoothing parameters and precision, so they should be fitted like the prediction model (REML), not merely "either is fine"; (c) note that gratia's `simulate()` draws responses at the fitted mean without coefficient uncertainty, so the intervals in those figures are response-spread intervals, not model-uncertainty intervals (a separate point for Andria).

5. *Recommendation.* Revert the seven to REML in both configurations (drop `method = comparison_method` at `scripts/4_calibration_model.R:84`, so the default `"REML"` of `R/model_registry.R:72` applies); rewrite `config.R:52-55`, `scripts/4_calibration_model.R:75-77` and `R/model_registry.R:68-71` to say: ladder by the comparison method so that all models compared by AIC and analysis of deviance share one smoothing-selection method (mgcv `anova.gam`, `gam.selection`), REML for every fit that is predicted or simulated from; record both decisions and the 15 October reversal in B4; take finding 5 to general question 7 with the concrete proposal; report the ML and REML January tables to Andria under B4.

Add: (i) a per-file invariant, `readRDS(f)$method` for all 204 fits equals the expected method (`"ML"` ladder, `"REML"` seven and prediction fit under `config.R`; all `"REML"` under legacy; `tools/check_calibration_month.R:26` already relies on this field), recorded in script 4's manifest; (ii) `SP_ELEV_LC` panel predictions within 1e-6 of the prediction fit's on the calibration table (or make them the same file); (iii) a parse-and-grep test that no `AIC`/`anova` call names a spatial-experiment object, so the comment cannot drift again; (iv) legacy run of script 4's seven loop byte-identical in the call (diff of the loop against `legacy`), since the fits themselves are not byte-comparable.
