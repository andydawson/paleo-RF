# Snow-free calibration as a first estimate of the snow effect: literature check by Fable

## Verdict

The building blocks of the proposal are standard; the exact comparison is not. Separating MODIS MCD43 retrievals into snow-free and snow-covered with the MCD43A2 snow flag, and building per-land-cover albedo statistics for each, is the normal practice (Moody 2005, 2007; Gao 2014; Barnes and Roy 2010; the NASA snow-free products MCD43C2 and MCD43GF are themselves made this way). What the published forcing studies then do is recombine the two with a snow-cover fraction, so the snow contribution comes out either as a decomposition (Jiao 2017) or as a snow-free-only sensitivity case against a snow-weighted case (Barnes and Roy 2008 versus 2010). I found no published study that estimates the snow contribution as "pipeline on all-days albedo minus pipeline on snow-free albedo". The nearest precedent is the Barnes and Roy pair: 2008 computed forcing from snow-free albedo only, 2010 added snow and snow-free albedo with snow data and reported that snow "exacerbated" the forcing. So the proposed two-run comparison is a defensible simplified variant of established practice, but a reviewer could ask why the snow term is left implicit in the all-days run rather than made explicit with a snow fraction, which is the established form and is also what the second test (shifting snow timing) needs.

## Question 2: months or regions with few or no snow-free retrievals

- NASA's own snow-free product: MCD43GF user-guide page (umb.edu/spectralmass, v006/v006.1, verified): "To generate a snow-free product, pixels with ephemeral snow were removed using the MCD43D41 snow flags"; "High quality observational data are used whenever possible"; "For pixels without high quality retrievals, a phenological/temporal curve fitting exercise is used to interpolate values"; "If the temporal fitting is unsuccessful, a spatial fit is used instead"; "If neither temporal nor spatial fitting work, spatial smoothing is used". Validation: Sun, Wang, Li, Erb, Schaaf (2017) Int. J. Appl. Earth Obs. Geoinf. 58:36-49, doi 10.1016/j.jag.2017.01.011 (verified).
- Moody et al. 2005 (above): "ecosystem-dependent temporal interpolation technique to fill missing or seasonally snow-covered data", phenological curves per IGBP class from MOD12Q1 (abstract). So winter snow-free albedo at high latitudes in these products is an interpolated, class-based value, not an observation.
- Gao et al. 2014: the multiscale hierarchy exists precisely so that class-months with too few samples fall back to a coarser scale; the threshold and rule: from memory, to be checked.
- Bright et al. 2015 (Biogeosciences, Section 2.2.1, verified): "High solar zenith angles precluded the number of sufficient MODIS retrievals in December; thus December mean biases were excluded"; Section 4 notes degraded atmospheric correction at solar zenith angles above 70 degrees in January and most of February. Excluding a month is an accepted answer.
- Wang, Schaaf, Sun, Shuai, Roman (2018) Capturing rapid land surface dynamics with Collection V006 MODIS BRDF/NBAR/Albedo (MCD43) products. Remote Sens. Environ. 207:50-64, doi 10.1016/j.rse.2018.02.001 (verified, NASA abstract): v006 uses all Terra and Aqua clear observations, improving high-latitude inversions. The MCD43A3 user-guide page (verified) advises using the band quality flags "to isolate the highest quality full inversion results".
- Barnes and Roy 2010 handling of winter months: not verified.
Implication for the pipeline: a January snow-free stream at 1 degree over northern North America will be dominated by southern and low-snow cells and by magnitude (backup) inversions; published practice either fills by class phenology, restricts to full inversions, or drops the month. Imposing a minimum-count mask per cell-month and reporting coverage would follow the MCD43GF logic.

## Question 3: cautions and a better-established simple alternative

Published: Barnes and Roy 2010 show that snow-free interannual variability alone changes the forcing materially (abstract), so part of any two-run difference is sampling and fit noise, not snow. Jiao 2017 (abstract) shows that in winter the snow-cover fraction, not the albedo contrast, controls the spatial pattern of forcing; a comparison that never varies the snow fraction cannot see this. Bright et al. 2015 (Biogeosciences, Section 4) caution that MODIS may underestimate cold dry snow albedo and that retrievals degrade at high solar zenith angles, so the snowy stream is the less certain one.

My own reading of the proposal (reasoning, not citation): the all-days minus snow-free difference is the snow contribution only at the modern snow climatology as sampled by MODIS clear days, with the implicit assumption of modern snow left in place in both runs; it does not say what the forcing would be under past snow. The snow-free run in winter is calibrated on a geographically biased subset and extrapolated north, so some of the difference is extrapolation. And the second test (shifting snow timing) has no handle in an all-days model.

The established simple alternative, which all three forcing papers above use: fit the monthly GAM twice, on snow-free and on snow-flagged albedo, and combine as albedo = f_snow x albedo_snow + (1 - f_snow) x albedo_snow_free, with f_snow the modern monthly snow-cover fraction per cell (MODIS MOD10 snow cover, or the share of snow-flagged retrievals in the all-days stream). Then forcing with f_snow = 0 is the snow-free case, f_snow = modern reproduces the all-days case, and the snow-timing test is just f_snow shifted by a month, which is the project lead's second test done inside the same model. The attribution (snow-free term, snow-covered term, f_snow) is then Jiao 2017's decomposition. Costs one extra fitted model per month and a snow-fraction input.

## Question 4: is the MCD43A2 snow flag the standard separation?

Yes. Verified wording:
- LAADS file specification, MCD43A2 Collection 6.1 (ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/filespec/MODIS/61/MCD43A2): Snow_BRDF_Albedo, "0 = Snow-free Albedo Retrieved", "1 = Snow Albedo Retrieved", 255 = fill.
- Google Earth Engine catalogue, MODIS/061/MCD43A2 (mirrors the LP DAAC description): "Snow-free or snow BRDF/albedo retrieved", "Bit 0: Mandatory QA; 0: Snow-free albedo retrieved; 1: Snow albedo retrieved".
- MCD43 v006/v006.1 User Guide, MCD43GF page (Schaaf and Wang, umb.edu/spectralmass): "To generate a snow-free product, pixels with ephemeral snow were removed using the MCD43D41 snow flags" (MCD43D41/D40 is the 30 arc-second version of the same flag; the earthdata record for MCD43D40 v061 says it "identifies each pixel as either 'Snow-free Albedo Retrieved' or 'Snow Albedo Retrieved'"). The guide also lists MCD43C2 "CMG BRDF/Albedo Model Snow-Free Parameters Product". NASA therefore builds its own snow-free products from this flag.
- Wang et al. 2018 (abstract, verified): Collection 6 "utilizes the day of interest snow/snow-free status to capture snow albedo", replacing the Collection 5 rule based on "the predominant snow/snow-free status of the majority of the observations over the 16-day retrieval period". That the inversion then uses only observations matching that status, so a flagged retrieval is a pure snow or pure snow-free retrieval: from memory, to be checked in the user guide PDF or Wang 2018 Section 2.

Note for the rebuild: with v6.1 daily retrievals, the "snowy days only" stream means days whose day-of-interest status was snow; a monthly all-days mean is then a snow-fraction-weighted mix of the two streams, which is why the blend in Question 3 reproduces it.
