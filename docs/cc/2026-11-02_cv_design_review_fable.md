# Fable (science-reviewer) review of Astra's cross-validation design, 2 November 2026

**Verdict.** Not ready to build as written: the selection is confounded by a spatial-separation penalty the hindcast never pays, which falls on exactly the term the candidates differ in; the tie-break and the eligibility of cover-free models tilt the same way; settle the task definition and the rule with Chris before any code.

**Benefit.** Choosing the albedo model by measured skill on cover mixes beyond the modern range, with spatial dependence accounted for, would let the paper state an error for its extrapolated hindcast cells (the northeast included) instead of only flagging them with a hull diagnostic.

**Hand check.**

1. Triangle distance (Astra step 2) against `R/training_support.R:31` (`px = ST + ET/2`, `py = ET*sqrt(3)/2`). p = (OL 0.5, ET 0.3, ST 0.2), q = (OL 0.4, ET 0.4, ST 0.2). Code: p → (0.35, 0.2598), q → (0.40, 0.3464); Euclidean distance √(0.05² + 0.0866²) = √(0.0025 + 0.0075) = 0.100. Astra's formula √((0.01 + 0.01 + 0)/2) = 0.100. Agree. In general Δpx = ΔST + ΔET/2, Δpy = ΔET√3/2, so d² = ΔST² + ΔST·ΔET + ΔET²; with ΔOL = −(ΔET + ΔST), (ΔOL² + ΔET² + ΔST²)/2 = the same. The formula is the project's existing geometry and is consistent with the hull distance `training_support()` computes with `st_distance` in the same plane.
2. "Every scored validation mixture is outside its training hull" (step 5): all training points satisfy u·p ≤ c, so does their convex hull; validation points have u·p > c. Agree.
3. Inverse-link remark (Astra's last paragraph of section 6). One cell, logit change +0.20 from a cover change. Baseline albedo 0.15 (η = −1.735): new = σ(−1.535) = 0.177, change +0.027. Another model putting the same cell's baseline at 0.25 (η = −1.099): new = σ(−0.899) = 0.289, change +0.039. Same logit change, 44% larger response-scale change. Remark correct, and it means models with identical cover terms but different location terms give different forcing.
4. December feasibility. 1,416 December cells with an all-days value (refit plan table, changelog line 1459). Cut at the 70th percentile → ~991; reserved blocks 15% plus guard bands, say 20% → ~793; one validation block plus buffer, say 10% → ~713 rows, below the 748 coefficients of models 7 and 8 (499 + 49 + 199 + intercept). At the 90th-percentile cut ~917 rows: script 4's floor passes, but a 748-coefficient beta GAM on ~900 rows is not a fit to select on. January (1,726): 70th → ~870; November (1,866): 70th → ~940. In winter the evergreen-rich tail is in the polar night, so the cut removes less training but the validation set for that direction is nearly empty. December is untestable for every direction; January and November are marginal at the 70th cut.
5. One-standard-error rule at Astra's floor of three blocks: SE = sd(d_b)/√3 from three paired block differences; the sd has two degrees of freedom, so its 95% sampling range is about 0.16× to 1.9× the true value. "Within one SE" is then decided by noise, and the rule degenerates to "simplest wins".
6. Counts. 5 × 11 × 12 = 660: correct, excluding retries, the 24 final fits and any control holdout. Script 4: 8 ladder fits (`scripts/4_calibration_model.R:46-55`) + 7 spatial (`:85-95`; `SPATIAL_MODELS` has seven ids, `R/model_registry.R:37-53`) + 1 prediction fit (`:103-112`) = 16 per month: correct. Three of the sixteen are the model-8 formula (ladder by ML; `spatial_elev_cover` and the prediction fit, both REML).

**Findings.**

F1. Blocker (design; Astra steps 3-5 and the "unavoidable mismatch" paragraph). The buffered geographic blocks make the validation task "new place and new cover". The hindcast's task is "same place, new cover": `scripts/6_prediction_model.R:45-47` predicts at the calibration cells' own x, y and elevation, and the questions file's entry "B3" (the spatial term cancels through time) states it. The spatial-extrapolation penalty across the buffer falls on the location term, and the candidates come in pairs that differ by exactly that term (mod8 vs `cover_elev`, `R/model_registry.R:33` vs `:49`; linear with vs without location). The selection therefore confounds "better cover response" with "pays no spatial-extrapolation penalty". Astra's own kNNDM citation says to match the CV's geographic distance distribution to the prediction task's; for this hindcast that distribution is a point mass at zero (Astra admits it), which argues against a buffer, not for one. Consequence: a no-location model could win for the wrong reason, and the paper's forcing and the northeast sign would change on that basis. Options (question 1 below): (a) no buffer: the global tail cut alone defines the holdout, the tail's own spatial clustering supplies the separation, and dependence is handled in the score's uncertainty by block resampling, not in the training set; (b) keep the blocks but score paired within-block differences (an above-cut cell minus a nearby below-cut cell held out in the same block): the spatial term's extrapolation cancels and the quantity is the modern analogue of the hindcast's slice difference; (c) add a no-cut control holdout and report the tail score relative to it, which measures the confound but does not remove it from the selection. Option (b) has a precedent to audit in the land-cover-albedo literature (local differences between neighbouring pixels of different cover: Duveiller, Hooker and Cescatti 2018, Nature Communications; Alkama and Cescatti 2016, Science; I cannot browse). Verification once decided: the fold table lists, for every validation cell, the distance to its nearest training cell, and the design states which task that distribution matches.

F2. Should-fix (selection rule, section 5). "Among comparable candidates, select the lowest average effective degrees of freedom": simplicity is defined after fitting by a data-dependent quantity, and the k = 500 location smooth dominates edf, so whenever scores are "comparable" the rule removes the location term. With F1 (location models penalised) and hand check 5 (wide SE at few blocks) the protocol has a structural tilt toward `cover_elev`, the one model already known from the 18 October raster run to flip the northeast sign (changelog lines 1360-1366: −0.095 with location, +0.043 without). That is not use of the sign to choose, but it is a protocol whose biases point at the known sign-flipper. Fix: pre-declare the simplicity order by coefficient count (`model_coefficients()`, `R/model_registry.R:64`) or a hand-written order; set a minimum number of blocks for the one-SE rule (Chris's number) and say what happens below it. Verification: a toy test of the rule on made-up scores with a known answer.

F3. Should-fix (candidates). Models 1-2 are eligible winners. If one wins, script 6's hindcast is constant across slices in every cell and the vegetation forcing is zero by construction. Astra says that is not evidence of zero forcing, but the selection would still have produced it. Suggest they are baselines (they answer "does cover add transferable skill?", a reportable result), not eligible as the prediction model. Chris's decision. Either way the demonstration should assert the invariant "a candidate with no cover term predicts zero change between any two slices".

F4. Should-fix (candidates). The list jumps from a 500-knot location smooth to none, and keeps the cover smooth at k = 200 (tp) or 75/200. The brief asked for "limits on the shape or flexibility of the cover terms, or different handling of the location term", and Astra's own citation (Paciorek 2010) says the scale of the spatial term, not its presence, governs the split with the covariate. Missing: one coarse location candidate (e.g. `s(x, y, k = 30)` with model 8's other terms) and one well-posed two-fraction cover smooth `s(ET, ST)` (option ii of the questions file's rank-deficiency entry "B1"), optionally with smaller k. Two more formulas, same fitting function. Without them the comparison cannot tell "location term too flexible" from "wrong to include".

F5. Should-fix (target versus score; steps 1 and section 5). The target population is all hindcast cell-slices, but the score is computed only over above-cut cells, i.e. the ~11% outside the hull (18 October run: 11.1%; 4.3%, 1.7%, 0.5% beyond 0.02, 0.05, 0.10). The chosen model then drives the ~89% in-range forcing with no in-range skill in the criterion; a linear cover model could win out-of-range and be worse in-range. Fix: one holdout with the same blocks and no cut, combined with the tail holdouts by the population's in/out shares; or state that selection is on the out-of-range part only and report in-range skill beside it. Also, target weights by hindcast share put nearly all weight on the 0-0.02 and 0.02-0.05 departure groups, where models differ least, so F2's tilt decides; Chris should choose the group weighting knowingly (question 5).

F6. Should-fix (what the holdout tests versus what the hemlock sign depends on; section 6). Direction families are departures from the hull (the level of the cover response outside the range); the hemlock sign is the gradient along evergreen-to-summergreen at very low open land (`tools/plotting/plot_northeast_cover_and_location.R:129-140`). A model can get the level right beyond the cut and the slope wrong. Add the within-validation diagnostic "signed error against evergreen share at fixed low open land", or state that the slope is untested, and phrase "what counts as evidence" in those terms. Note too that the 6 ka northeast mixes have less open land than any modern cell (changelog 1365-1366), so the relevant tail holdout is the lowest-open-land modern cells, i.e. the northeast's own; under that holdout the northeast is predicted as unseen territory, F1 in its sharpest form.

F7. Should-fix (feasibility pre-declared; step 7). Hand check 4. Astra leaves the untestable months to be found during the run. Declare the testable months and directions from script 2's run-record counts before any fit, in the frozen protocol, and say that "one structure for all months" rests on the testable months and is imposed on the rest.

F8. Should-fix (failed fits; section 5). Model 7 reached its 500-step limit in several months on the full table (changelog, model-7 entries). The design retries once and forbids dropping failed folds, but does not say what a candidate's score is if a fold still fails: ineligible, or scored from the unconverged fit with a flag. An unconverged `bam()` still predicts, so pre-specify.

F9. Should-fix (simplicity; Chris's rule of 1 November). Heavier than the goal needs: (i) reserved 15% blocks plus guard bands, removed from all 660 fits with their own bookkeeping, buy a safeguard against optimism among eleven pre-specified candidates under a pre-specified rule, a modest risk; drop them and call the development score a selection score, unless Chris wants the extra guarantee; (ii) three cut options with a percentile-matching rule: pre-declare one cut (80th) and move only if infeasible; (iii) with a global cut, the development validation set can be all above-cut cells; the geographic validation blocks exist only to carry the buffer (F1) and the "different blocks per holdout" allocation; (iv) "distinct predictor configurations" and rank checks duplicate script 4's row floor (`:36-40`) and mgcv's rank report; (v) per-group "represented target share" bookkeeping can be one table printed once. Keep: block resampling, monthly hulls, identical rows across candidates, scoring predicted means. Astra's code-shape table is right provided `R/cross_validation.R` stays at three functions: build the holdout table, score, select.

**Literature (review question 6; identities not verified online).** Roberts 2017, Valavi 2019, Milà 2022, Linnenbrink 2024, Meyer and Pebesma 2021, King and Zeng 2006, Cribari-Neto and Zeileis 2010, Paciorek 2010, Pya and Wood 2015, Yates 2023, Lahiri 2003, Cawley and Talbot 2010: titles, venues and DOIs as given look right to me. **What the proposed verification misses.** Membership invariants per holdout and month: no training row above the cut; no training row inside a buffer (if kept); no reserved row in any development fit; every validation row has that month's albedo; the same validation rows for every candidate; `training_support(validation, training_hull(training))$outside` all TRUE. Unit test of the distance function against hand check 1. A toy test of the selection rule. Zero-change invariant for cover-free candidates. Determinism: the same holdout table from the same seed; a repeated fold fit byte-identical. Weight bookkeeping: group weights sum to the represented share; a cell in two holdouts has its weight split. Propagation: after selection, `config.R` `model_id` yields a REML full-data fit of the chosen id and scripts 5-10 run on it in a separate run directory; the northeast tool's model-8 stop is handled.

**Questions for Chris or Andria** (meanwhile the code does nothing: design only).
1. Which task should the folds imitate: new place and new cover (Astra's buffered blocks; conservative; confounded for the location question), same place and new cover (no buffer; tail cut only), or the paired within-block difference score (closest to the slice difference; precedent to audit)? My recommendation: the paired difference as the selection score, computed on Astra's blocks or on the unbuffered tail, with absolute-albedo error reported beside it.
2. Are models 1-2 eligible winners or baselines only? Recommendation: baselines.
3. Keep the reserved blocks? Recommendation: drop; report the development score as a selection score.
4. Add an in-range holdout and weight in/out by the population shares? Recommendation: yes, one holdout.
5. Weight departure groups by hindcast share (near-edge dominates) or equally (extrapolation dominates)? Recommendation: by share for the headline, equal as a reported sensitivity.
6. Add a coarse-location candidate and a two-fraction cover smooth? Recommendation: both.
7. Which months are testable: declared from script 2's counts before any fit? Recommendation: yes; December expected untestable.
8. Equal month weights, or sunlight-weighted? Recommendation: equal for the selection, as script 9 averages months equally; note the limitation.
