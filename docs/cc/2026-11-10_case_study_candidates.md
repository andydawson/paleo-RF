# Candidate case studies for testing the albedo model: a ranked shortlist

Written 10 November 2026 by a Claude search agent, for Chris. A search result, not reviewed: every number below was computed from the three finished runs by the scripts in this folder, and every statement about past vegetation or about albedo carries the source and the passage read. Where only an abstract could be read, the source list says so.

Words used throughout. "ka" is thousand years before present (present = 1950). A change is always the younger slice minus the older one. An albedo change is positive when the surface gets brighter. A forcing is positive when it warms (the surface got darker), as in the pipeline. The three runs are "model 8" (the original), the "chosen model" (location term of 100, cover as straight lines) and the "coarse-location model" (model 8 with a location term of 30). The three cover classes are open land, evergreen (trees and shrubs) and summergreen (trees and shrubs).

## 1. Conclusion

1. **The hemlock decline is one of a family, and it is not the best member.** Wherever the reconstruction swaps evergreen for summergreen cover, the three runs behave as they do in the north-east: model 8 makes the surface brighter in spring and summer when evergreen replaces summergreen (the opposite of every published contrast), the chosen model gives the published sign and size in spring and summer but the opposite sign in winter, and the coarse-location model gives the published sign with a large size. This holds in six cases in five regions (table in section 3.1). Two of them are bigger than the hemlock decline and lie mostly inside the range of modern cover mixes, where the hemlock decline lies almost wholly outside it: spruce replacing alder and birch in Quebec-Labrador (6 to 2.5 ka) and spruce replacing deciduous woodland and shrubs in north-west Canada (10 to 8 ka). The disagreement between the runs in north-west Canada alone is as large as their whole disagreement over the vegetation forcing of 8 to 10 ka, and Quebec-Labrador is the largest regional disagreement of 4 to 6 ka (section 3.2).
2. **The best new test is the land clearance at European settlement in the eastern United States** (the 500 BP to 50 BP slices). What happened is fixed by written history, not by pollen; a published estimate of the albedo change exists for the very same change; there is no ice and the least climate change of any candidate; and the younger end is the state the model was fitted to. All three runs brighten the surface, as expected, but they differ threefold in size, and in summer two of them give almost nothing.
3. **Where open land is exchanged for trees and shrubs, the three runs agree in sign with each other and with the published contrasts, in every case found** (land clearance, the prairie's advance and retreat in the Midwest, the greening of Alaska and north-west Canada, the treeline's retreat, the closing of the Pacific Northwest forest). These cases test size and season, not sign.
4. **I would look first at three, beside the hemlock decline:** the land clearance; the Quebec-Labrador spruce rise with north-west Canada as its second region; and the prairie-forest border in the Midwest. Together with the hemlock decline they cover both kinds of exchange, with and without snow, inside and outside the range of modern mixes.
5. **A warning from the search:** in two of the three runs (model 8 and the coarse-location model) the South-west supplies much of the continental vegetation forcing between 6 and 0.5 ka, and the Southern Plains add to it in places, while the chosen model gives little there. Both regions have very few pollen cells and their cover jumps between slices by more than the changes of interest (section 6). They are not test cases; they are places where the result rests on the interpolation.

- **Where they disagree most:** the largest entries in italics that I traced to a cover change are evergreen-for-summergreen exchanges (north-west Canada, Quebec-Labrador, the north-east, the Ohio valley and Great Lakes in the early Holocene); the others lie in the South-west and the Southern Plains.

## 4. The three I would look at first, and why

**First: the land clearance at European settlement, eastern United States (candidate 1).** It is the only case where the vegetation change is known from something other than pollen, and the only one with a published albedo estimate for the same place and change. It has no ice sheet near it, the smallest climate difference from today, and its younger end is the modern state. It tests the open-land contrast, which drives most of the forcing of the last 500 years in all three runs, in snowy and snow-free months. It also has a built-in second test: the clearance reaches the Atlantic seaboard by the 200 BP slice (1600 to 1850) and the interior only by the 50 BP slice, as the history says it should.

**Second: the spruce rise in Quebec-Labrador (candidate 2), with north-west Canada (candidate 5) as its second region.** It is the hemlock decline's mirror image (evergreen gained instead of lost), almost perfectly clean (cleanness 0.95), about as large (0.18 of cover), on more than twice the cells, after nearly all the ice had gone, and four-fifths of it lies inside the range of modern mixes. A wrong sign there cannot be put down to extrapolation, as it can in the north-east. The expected sign is surest in the snow season, which at these latitudes is also when the forcing is made (March to June). North-west Canada repeats the test 4,000 years earlier on the other side of the continent, with a larger change, and it is where the 8 to 10 ka disagreement comes from; it carries more risk (early Holocene climate, few pollen cells).

**Third: the prairie-forest border in the Midwest (candidate 4), advance (10.5 to 8 ka) and retreat (4.5 to 0.5 ka).** It is the classic Holocene example of a moving vegetation boundary, with an independent pollen-based reconstruction of woody cover that gives the same timing; its mixes are familiar today (at most 5 % of the area outside modern mixes in the advance); and it lies where snow covers the ground in winter today, so it tests the snow-season contrast between grass and forest. The runs agree in sign there, so it is a check on size and season: for example, model 8 gives no summer brightening for the advance, where grass replacing spruce should brighten the surface most surely.

The hemlock decline stays a good case for what it tests (dense pollen data, a signal seven standard deviations above the reconstruction's uncertainty, a climate not far from today's). Its two weaknesses are that the expected effect is small (+0.004 to +0.013) and that nearly every cell is outside the range of modern mixes at both ends: the pre-settlement north-east had more summergreen cover and less open land than any cell has today (at 4.5 ka, 64 % of its cells exceed the highest summergreen fraction of any modern cell, 0.71, and 54 % have less open land than any modern cell). The land clearance is the reason: it removed those mixes from the modern landscape.

## 6. Looked at and not recommended, and other things that jump out

- **Southern Plains, 4 to 2 ka** (103 to 94 W, 26 to 40 N; 111 cells). Open land falls by 0.115, but the standard deviation is 0.085 and the 95 % interval runs from -0.27 to +0.04; only 8 or 9 cells have pollen data; from one slice to the next the box's open land moves with a standard deviation of 0.05. Model 8 makes +5.1 W/m2 of it (+11.3 mW/m2 global equivalent, the second largest regional entry of 2 to 4 ka after the South-west), the chosen model +1.1 and the coarse-location model -0.1. The forcing's sign changes when the box or the window is moved (it holds in 50 to 78 % of variants).
- **South-west** (115 to 103 W, 28 to 38 N; 114 cells; 14 with pollen data). Evergreen is 0.29 at 10 ka, 0.57 at 9.5 ka and 0.32 at 9 ka: a jump at a single slice, the largest slice-to-slice change anywhere in the reconstruction, reversed at the next slice. A lasting opening follows between 7 and 5 ka (open land +0.236, standard deviation 0.111). Dawson et al. 2025 (section 3.3.4) say dry-land vegetation is "likely to be under-represented" for lack of sites and that bare ground cannot be reconstructed. In section 3.2 the South-west contributes -11.6, -12.2, +16.4 and -15.6 mW/m2 in model 8 over the four periods from 8 to 0.5 ka, -1.1, -15.0, +16.1 and -7.6 in the coarse-location model, and -4.5, -0.1, +0.3 and -0.7 in the chosen model.
- **South-east.** The best-known change there, the rise of pine about 5,000 radiocarbon years ago (Watts 1971, abstract: before it "sclerophyllous oak forest, scrub, or savanna"; after it "pine forest came to predominate"), hardly shows: in Florida and southern Georgia (88 to 79 W, 27 to 33 N; 28 cells) evergreen rises from about 0.10 to about 0.20 and summergreen stays the largest class throughout. The box series is noisy (open land moves by 0.06 to 0.10 between slices). Not usable as a test; it raises a question for Andria (section 8).
- **Eastern Arctic** (90 to 65 W, 62 to 73 N). Summergreen cover falls by 0.10 in the last step (200 BP to 50 BP) alone; few sites; no winter fitting cells. Not pursued.
- **Hudson Bay lowlands, 7.5 to 7 ka** (90 to 80 W, 52 to 57 N). Evergreen rises by 0.42 in one step, soon after the ice went (Dawson et al. 2025, section 3.3.2, date the collapse of the Laurentide ice to 8.4 ka). Not checked against the literature.
- **How the search was made.** Every tile of 10 by 5 degrees (stepped by half a tile) and every pair of slices up to 2,000 years apart was ranked by the size of the cover change, by how clean an exchange it was, by the three runs' agreement and by the forcing (`07_search_tiles_and_windows.R`; maps by period in `figures/`). The candidates of section 5 are the features that are both in that ranking and in the literature; the features above are in the ranking only.

## 7. How the numbers were made

**Files, and what each column was taken to mean** (checked against the pipeline's scripts 6, 7a and 8 in the worktree).

- `data/lct_paleo_reveals_interp.RDS`: one row per cell and slice. `ages` is the slice in years BP; `x`, `y` the cell centre; `OL`, `ET`, `ST` the cover fractions (the mean of 200 posterior draws). Identical in the three runs. All 2,655 study-area cells have all 25 slices.
- `output/prediction/paleo_interp_predict_gam_<month>_bluesky.RDS`: `year` is the slice; `alb_mean` is the model's fitted albedo for that cell, slice and month (no simulation). The albedo changes in this report are differences of these.
- `data/ALB_diffs_bluesky.RDS` and `output/forcing/RF_holocene_all_cases.RDS`: one row per cell, month and pair of consecutive slices; `year` is the younger slice; `ice_frac_young` and `ice_frac_old` the cell's ice fraction at the two ends; `alb_diff_veg_part` the albedo change (from the mean of 100 simulated draws, not the fitted value) times the ice-free share at the older end; `rf_<kernel>_veg_part` that change times the kernel, in W/m2, positive for warming. The forcing in this report is the sum of `rf_hadgem_veg_part` over the pairs of a window, averaged over the months with a value and over the box by cell area. The fitted and the simulated albedo changes differ by at most 0.005 in any candidate, month and run.
- `data/calibration_modern_lct_interp_bluesky.RDS`: the modern cells with their cover and observed monthly albedo (`jan` to `dec`); used for the range of modern mixes, for the observed albedo of each box and for the share of its cells observed in each month.
- From the worktree's `data` folder, two inputs of the pipeline that are not in the run folders: `veg_posts_interp_ice.RDS`, the 200 posterior draws of the cover (for the reconstruction's own uncertainty), and `lct_paleo_reveals.RDS`, the pollen-based cover before interpolation (for where the pollen data are).

**Definitions.**

- A box includes its west and south edges and not its east and north edges, as in `R/regions.R`. A cell counts by its area times its ice-free share at the icier end of the window.
- The reconstruction's uncertainty: each posterior draw is a draw of the whole map of one slice (neighbouring cells' draws correlate at 0.8) and the slices are independent of each other (the same draw number at two slices correlates at 0.00), so the standard deviation of a box-mean change is taken over draw-by-draw differences. A single cell's cover has a posterior standard deviation of 0.08 to 0.10, so single cells say little; box means of some tens of cells say more. This uncertainty covers the interpolation only: Dawson et al. 2025 (section 4.3) state that it leaves out the errors of the pollen-to-cover step and of the dating.
- "Outside the range of modern mixes" uses the project's own rule (`training_hull()` and `training_support()` of `R/training_support.R`): outside the convex hull of the modern cells' mixes for the month of May; distances are on the cover triangle, where 0.05 is about five percentage points of cover.
- The expected albedo change is the cover change of each class times a published albedo of that class, summed. It assumes that albedo mixes in proportion to cover and that the published classes stand for the reconstruction's classes; it is a guide to sign and rough size, no more. The class albedos are in `08_candidates.R` and `candidate_expectation.csv`.
- "12-month mean" gives each month the same weight. At high latitudes the winter months have little or no sunlight and few or no fitting cells; the forcing, which weights each month by the kernel, is the better summary there.

**Checks.** The north-east box reproduces the project's comparison trial (January changes to four decimal places; the outside-range shares of 0.98 and 1.00). The twelve regions of section 3.2 add up to each run's own period figures. The mean of the posterior draws is exactly the cover the runs used. Moving each box by two degrees and each window by one slice leaves the sign of every run's forcing unchanged in every ranked case except the Pacific Northwest (model 8, 94 % of variants) and the Quebec-Labrador opening (model 8, 83 %); `candidate_sensitivity.csv`. The boxes and slices were drawn from the literature and from the reconstruction's cover series, not adjusted to the runs' results; I had however already seen those results in the search tables when I drew them, so this check is the safeguard.
